# Classifications and markings

Every page carries a **protective marking** — a classification level, an
optional eyes-only caveat, and a national prefix. It appears as a banner at the
top and bottom of the page, so it travels with the text if the page is copied,
printed or screenshotted.

## What the levels mean

Four, in order:

1. **OFFICIAL**
2. **OFFICIAL-SENSITIVE**
3. **SECRET**
4. **TOP SECRET**

You can read a page if your clearance is at or above its level, and — if the
page carries an eyes-only caveat — if your nationality is one of the ones
listed.

## Why you sometimes see less than a colleague

A classification only ever **subtracts**. Being a space admin, or even an
instance admin, does not read around a marking: administration and clearance
are separate things.

So two people looking at the same space can legitimately see different pages,
different search results, and different totals in analytics. That is not an
inconsistency or a bug. It is the whole mechanism working.

You will not be told what you are missing. A page above your clearance does not
appear as a locked row — it is simply not in the tree, not in search, and not
in any count. A count of hidden things would itself disclose how much
classified material exists, so nothing anywhere reports one.

## Setting a marking

Editors set a page's marking on its **Details** screen. Two rules apply:

- a new page inherits its parent's marking, so a child is never accidentally
  less protected than the page it sits under;
- **you cannot set a marking you would not then be able to read.** Otherwise it
  would be possible to write a page and immediately lose access to it.

Lowering a marking widens who can read something, so it is recorded distinctly
in the audit log from raising one.
